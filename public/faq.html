P2P Scams: How to Stay Protected

Your comprehensive FAQ guide to preventing cyber cell bank freezes, spotting fraudulent buyers, and trading crypto securely in India.

Why do bank accounts get frozen after P2P trades in India?

When you sell USDT/USDC on a standard exchange, the buyer might transfer rupees using a compromised or hacked bank account. When the victim files a police complaint with the cyber cell, the authorities place debit freezes on all recipient accounts in the transaction trail.

P2PFather mitigates this by fostering direct peer-to-peer verification, requiring identity matching, and coordinating through an audited smart contract escrow on Base and BSC.

How does P2PFather prevent P2P scams?

P2PFather operates as a decentralized, Telegram-native escrow protocol with key security safeguards:

  • Audited Escrow Smart Contracts: Crypto is held on-chain on Base/BSC—not in a centralized custodial server.
  • Verified Accounts: Users coordinate via verified Telegram handles with public trade histories and trust scores.
  • Live Admin Mediation: Active trade disputes can be reviewed by human administrators who verify banking receipts and UTRs before funds are released.

What should I do if my bank account receives a lien or freeze?

Follow these structured steps immediately:

  1. Contact your bank's branch manager and request the official Cyber Cell block notice containing the police station name, Investigating Officer (IO), and FIR reference number.
  2. Download the official P2PFather chat logs, trade agreement, and bank transaction reference (UTR).
  3. Submit a formal written representation to the investigating authority proving that you received funds as a legitimate seller in a peer-to-peer digital transaction.
⚠️
Never pay unverified third parties claiming to unlock accounts. Always demand official notice letters and communicate directly with authorized cyber police departments.

What are the common red flags of a P2P scammer?

  • Mismatched Account Names: The sender's UPI/bank name does not match their verified Telegram name.
  • External Chat Requests: Asking to settle off-platform without locking crypto in the escrow vault.
  • Fake SMS/Screenshots: Demanding quick crypto release using forged bank SMS or edited payment receipts.
  • Urgent Rush Tactics: Pressuring you to release before you have logged into your bank app to confirm the INR balance.
